Artifact 1

Stakeholder Communication Translator (AI Chatbot)

An AI-powered chatbot I designed and built to help technical professionals translate complex AI/ML concepts into clear, business-relevant language for non-technical stakeholders.

View live demo →
Description

This chatbot takes a technical concept or project update as input, identifies who the audience is, then produces a jargon-free explanation paired with a specific line on why the concept matters to that audience's goals (revenue, efficiency, risk, etc.), and offers to adjust the explanation further.

It was designed and tested using a design thinking framework (Empathy, Problem, Ideation, Prototype, Test) in ChatGPT, then deployed as a live, publicly accessible agent on Chatbase.co.

Objective

To address a real, recurring problem in AI/ML adoption: technical teams often explain what is changing without explaining why it matters, which stalls stakeholder buy-in. This project let me apply change-leadership research directly to a working tool.

Process

I identified the problem through empathy-mapping who is affected (technical staff communicating with non-technical stakeholders), defined a clear problem statement, considered multiple bot ideas before selecting this one, planned the bot's behavior across ten design questions, wrote a structured system prompt using a Context/Task/Constraints framework, and tested it against two different audiences (a VP of Sales and a warehouse operations manager) to confirm it adapted its language and business framing correctly.

Artifact 2

Adaptive Learning in Change Leadership: A Case Analysis

A written reflection analyzing a real workplace change initiative through the lens of adaptive learning, paired with an AI-generated hypothetical scenario exploring the same principle in an AI/ML integration context.

Description

This piece examines why change initiatives stall when teams commit to a rigid plan instead of adjusting course as new information emerges. It draws on a real Agile/DevOps rollout I was part of, then extends the analysis using an AI-generated hypothetical scenario to explore how the same principle plays out during AI/ML technology adoption.

Objective

To apply adaptive learning theory, drawn from change leadership research, to a real organizational change I experienced, and to demonstrate how the same failure pattern shows up in AI/ML rollouts, where teams often treat deployment as a finish line rather than the start of a feedback loop.

Process

I identified a workplace initiative where my team implemented Agile methodology in Azure DevOps using a standard framework, without adjusting for how our own applications and tasks were actually structured. Early signs that the approach wasn't fitting were not acted on, which led to significant rework once the mismatch became unavoidable. I analyzed this experience against adaptive learning principles, specifically continuous monitoring, willingness to experiment, and modifying plans based on feedback, to identify what a more adaptive response would have looked like. I then used AI (Claude, by Anthropic) to generate a parallel hypothetical scenario involving an AI-powered scheduling rollout in healthcare, to test whether the same adaptive-learning pattern held in a technology-adoption context, and disclosed that AI use directly in the piece.

Artifact 3

Explaining Neural Networks Simply: A Communication-for-Learning Exercise

A discussion post applying communication-for-learning strategies, chunking, analogy, and progressive disclosure, to translate a complex AI/ML concept into a plain-language explanation for a non-technical audience.

Description

Given the challenge of explaining neural networks as simply as possible, I worked with a generative AI tool to draft and refine an explanation built around a relatable analogy (recognizing cats vs. dogs in photos), then structured it using established communication-for-learning principles rather than presenting it as a raw technical definition.

Objective

To practice a skill directly relevant to my value proposition: taking a technical AI/ML concept and making it genuinely accessible to a non-technical audience, not just simplified, but structured in a way that actually supports learning and retention.

Process

I studied communication-for-learning strategies, including chunking information, using analogy and storytelling, and progressive disclosure (introducing foundational concepts before technical detail). I then prompted a generative AI tool (Claude, by Anthropic) to draft a plain-language explanation of neural networks, and iterated on the prompt to apply these specific strategies rather than accepting a generic technical answer. The final piece breaks the concept into three clearly labeled layers (input, hidden, output) introduced only after the reader has an intuitive mental model to attach them to.
